Prioritise: run the deciding studies first.

BioSim suggests an order for your studies that surfaces a programme-ending problem as early, and as cheaply, as possible.

The idea

Find the deal-breaker before the expensive work.

A programme moves forward only if every study passes. So the studies most likely to stop it, for the time they take, should run first. This is a well-studied problem in sequential testing, with a known best answer.1

BioSim estimates, for your molecule, which liabilities are most likely: from what approved drugs in the same class showed in animals, from the molecule’s chemistry and from hERG data on close analogues. It then orders the studies by time divided by their score for finding a programme-ending problem, always inside the order the guidelines require. A dose range-finding study still comes before the pivotal study it informs.

Worked example

An oral kinase inhibitor.

Illustrative inputs. The kill score ranks studies; it is not a calibrated probability. In BioSim it comes from the molecule’s own triage, and durations come from your lab quotes.

  4. 28-day GLP repeat-dose toxicity18 weeks0.92
  5. Dog cardiovascular telemetry12 weeks0.36
  6. Ames and in vitro micronucleus7 weeks0.03

Limits

Decision support, stated as such.

The suggested order never adds, removes or waives a required study, and it never enters a submission. It claims no accuracy until a pre-registered validation against known programme outcomes is published. Without your lab quotes, BioSim uses a flagged default duration and marks the order provisional.
