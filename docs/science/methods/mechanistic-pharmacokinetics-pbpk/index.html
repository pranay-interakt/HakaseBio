Methods registry

Mechanistic pharmacokinetics (PBPK) methods.

9 methods in this area. Each lists the assumptions it makes and the limits outside which its result is not valid.

pbpk.cl_allometric_scaling · version 1.0.0

Clearance projected between species by simple allometry on body weight

Assumptions

  • clearance scales with body weight to the power 0.75, so clearance per kilogram scales as body weight to the power -0.25
  • body weights are read from the cited physiology table, not from literals

Limits

  • simple allometry carries no information about the target species' enzymes or transporters, so a species-divergent metabolic route is not represented
  • it is used only where the target species has no measured clearance
  • no accuracy is claimed until a pre-registered, held-out validation is published for the projection

References

  • Boxenbaum H. Interspecies scaling, allometry, physiological time, and the ground plan of pharmacokinetics. J Pharmacokinet Biopharm. 1982;10(2):201-227. doi.org/10.1007/BF01062336
  • Mahmood I, Balian JD. The pharmacokinetic principles behind scaling from preclinical results to phase I protocols. Clin Pharmacokinet. 1999;36(1):1-11. doi.org/10.2165/00003088-199936010-00001

pbpk.cl_renal_filtration · version 1.0.0

Renal clearance as glomerular filtration of the unbound drug

Assumptions

  • the drug is filtered and neither secreted nor reabsorbed

Limits

  • a lower bound on renal clearance: active secretion raises it and tubular reabsorption lowers it, and neither is predicted here
  • refused for a molecule above the glomerular filtration cut-off

References

  • Davies B, Morris T. Physiological parameters in laboratory animals and humans. Pharm Res. 1993;10(7):1093-1095. doi.org/10.1023/A:1018943613122

pbpk.minimal_model · version 1.0.0

Minimal physiologically based model: blood, liver, rapidly and slowly equilibrating tissue

Assumptions

  • each compartment is well stirred and perfusion-limited
  • hepatic elimination follows the well-stirred model on unbound blood concentration
  • distribution is linear: no saturable tissue binding over the simulated range

Limits

  • a reduced structure: it predicts plasma exposure, not tissue-specific exposure in any organ other than the liver
  • linear kinetics only; saturable metabolism, transporter involvement and enterohepatic recycling are not represented and are flagged when suspected

References

  • Cao Y, Jusko WJ. Applications of minimal physiologically-based pharmacokinetic models. J Pharmacokinet Pharmacodyn. 2012;39(6):711-723. doi.org/10.1007/s10928-012-9280-2
  • Rowland M, Peck C, Tucker G. Physiologically-based pharmacokinetics in drug development and regulatory science. Annu Rev Pharmacol Toxicol. 2011;51:45-73. doi.org/10.1146/annurev-pharmtox-010510-100540
  • Wilkinson GR, Shand DG. Commentary: a physiological approach to hepatic drug clearance. Clin Pharmacol Ther. 1975;18(4):377-390. doi.org/10.1002/cpt1975184377

pbpk.monte_carlo · version 1.0.0

Parameter uncertainty propagated by Monte Carlo sampling

Assumptions

  • each uncertain input is sampled from the distribution the caller declared, independently unless a correlation is supplied

Limits

  • propagates only the uncertainty that was declared; it does not discover structural error in the model
  • the seed is recorded so a run is reproducible exactly

References

  • Metropolis N, Ulam S. The Monte Carlo method. J Am Stat Assoc. 1949;44(247):335-341. doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1949.10483310
  • ICH M15: General Principles for Model-Informed Drug Development. Step 4 final guideline, adopted 29 January 2026; adopted by FDA as final guidance June 2026. database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_M15_Step4_Final_Guideline_2026_0129.pdf

pbpk.ode_solution · version 1.0.0

Numerical solution of the compartmental system

Assumptions

  • the system is non-stiff at the tolerances used, with LSODA as fallback

Limits

  • solution carries the solver tolerance; the mass-balance residual and the analytic cross-check are reported with every run

References

  • Dormand JR, Prince PJ. A family of embedded Runge-Kutta formulae. J Comput Appl Math. 1980;6(1):19-26. doi.org/10.1016/0771-050X(80)90013-3
  • Hindmarsh AC. ODEPACK, a systematized collection of ODE solvers. In: Scientific Computing. North-Holland; 1983:55-64. (LSODA, the stiff/non-stiff switching solver exposed by SciPy as method='LSODA'.)

pbpk.oral_absorption · version 1.0.0

Oral bioavailability as the product of fraction absorbed, gut and hepatic availability

Assumptions

  • F = Fa x Fg x Fh with first-order appearance into the portal circulation

Limits

  • Fa from permeability and solubility is a classification, not a measurement
  • no transit-time, precipitation or food-effect model; a low-solubility compound is flagged rather than assigned a number

References

  • Amidon GL, Lennernas H, Shah VP, Crison JR. A theoretical basis for a biopharmaceutic drug classification: the correlation of in vitro drug product dissolution and in vivo bioavailability. Pharm Res. 1995;12(3):413-420. doi.org/10.1023/a:1016212804288
  • Yu LX, Amidon GL. A compartmental absorption and transit model for estimating oral drug absorption. Int J Pharm. 1999;186(2):119-125. doi.org/10.1016/s0378-5173(99)00147-7
  • Wilkinson GR, Shand DG. Commentary: a physiological approach to hepatic drug clearance. Clin Pharmacol Ther. 1975;18(4):377-390. doi.org/10.1002/cpt1975184377

pbpk.physiology · version 1.0.0

Species physiological parameter scaled from a reference animal

Assumptions

  • the per-kilogram value is the tabulated absolute value divided by the reference body weight stated in that table's column heading

Limits

  • a reference-animal mean, not a strain-, sex- or age-specific value
  • scaling a flow linearly with body weight within a species is an approximation; the allometric alternative is offered where a table supports it

References

  • Davies B, Morris T. Physiological parameters in laboratory animals and humans. Pharm Res. 1993;10(7):1093-1095. doi.org/10.1023/A:1018943613122
  • Brown RP, Delp MD, Lindstedt SL, Rhomberg LR, Beliles RP. Physiological parameter values for physiologically based pharmacokinetic models. Toxicol Ind Health. 1997;13(4):407-484. doi.org/10.1177/074823379701300401

pbpk.vss_oie_tozer · version 1.0.0

Steady-state volume of distribution from plasma and tissue unbound fractions

Assumptions

  • drug distributes into plasma water, extracellular water and a remainder compartment, with binding to extravascular protein in fixed proportion to plasma

Limits

  • the published parameter set is human; applying it to another species requires that species' fluid volumes and is refused without them

References

  • Oie S, Tozer TN. Effect of altered plasma protein binding on apparent volume of distribution. J Pharm Sci. 1979;68(9):1203-1205. doi.org/10.1002/jps.2600680948

pbpk.vss_single_species_scaling · version 1.0.0

Steady-state volume of distribution projected between species by unbound fraction

Assumptions

  • tissue affinity for the unbound drug is conserved across species, so Vss/fu is the invariant

Limits

  • fails for a drug with a species-divergent transporter or tissue-binding protein
  • reported with the observed fold-error of the method on its own validation set where one exists; otherwise the projection is refused

References

  • Obach RS, Baxter JG, Liston TE, et al. The prediction of human pharmacokinetic parameters from preclinical and in vitro metabolism data. J Pharmacol Exp Ther. 1997;283(1):46-58.
  • Oie S, Tozer TN. Effect of altered plasma protein binding on apparent volume of distribution. J Pharm Sci. 1979;68(9):1203-1205. doi.org/10.1002/jps.2600680948
