biologic.assembled_extinction_280 · version 1.0.0

Molar absorption coefficient at 280 nm of a multi-chain protein: the sum over chain copies plus 125 per inter-chain disulfide bond

Assumptions

  • absorbance in water at 280 nm; the folded, fully oxidised molecule

Limits

  • non-protein chromophores, including a conjugated payload or a dye, are not counted

References

  • Pace CN, Vajdos F, Fee L, Grimsley G, Gray T. How to measure and predict the molar absorption coefficient of a protein. Protein Sci. 1995;4(11):2411-2423. doi.org/10.1002/pro.5560041120
  • Liu H, May K. Disulfide bond structures of IgG molecules: structural variations, chemical modifications and possible impacts to stability and biological function. MAbs. 2012;4(1):17-23. doi.org/10.4161/mabs.4.1.18347

biologic.assembled_mass · version 1.0.0

Average mass of a multi-chain protein: the sum over chain copies less two hydrogen atoms per disulfide bond

Assumptions

  • the disulfide topology is declared by the sponsor, or taken from the antibody isotype only when every chain's cysteine count matches it
  • Liu & May 2012 state 12 intra-chain bonds per IgG and the hinge count per subclass; the split of those 12 into four per heavy chain and two per light chain follows from the domain architecture and is not quoted from them

Limits

  • glycans, C-terminal lysine processing and other post-translational modification are not included, so a glycoprotein's product mass is higher

References

  • SIB Swiss Institute of Bioinformatics, ExPASy. Protein Identification Tools - Mass values used (FindMod): average and monoisotopic amino-acid residue masses and water. web.expasy.org/findmod/findmod_masses.html
  • Liu H, May K. Disulfide bond structures of IgG molecules: structural variations, chemical modifications and possible impacts to stability and biological function. MAbs. 2012;4(1):17-23. doi.org/10.4161/mabs.4.1.18347
  • Prohaska T, Irrgeher J, Benefield J, et al. Standard atomic weights of the elements 2021 (IUPAC Technical Report). Pure Appl Chem. 2022;94(5):573-600. doi.org/10.1515/pac-2019-0603

biologic.sequence_liabilities · version 1.0.0

Chemical-degradation and glycosylation motifs located in a protein sequence

Assumptions

  • the registered one-letter sequence is the expressed sequence
  • disulfide pairing is as declared by the sponsor; cysteines beyond the declared pairs are reported as free thiols
  • the variable/constant boundary is assigned only when a human constant-region sequence matches exactly, and is otherwise left unassigned
  • the canonical motif set is the one Lu et al. 2019 name for clinical-stage antibodies; the relative rates within it are the pentapeptide rates measured by Robinson & Robinson 2001, and a motif they did not measure is reported without a rate rather than ranked

Limits

  • a motif is a liability only where the residue is solvent-exposed or in a binding site; neither follows from the sequence, so no motif here is a defect
  • Lu et al. found that counting motifs over-calls heavily: most motifs in clinical antibodies do not degrade measurably
  • the rates are from model pentapeptides; the formulation and the local structure decide the rate in a real product
  • proline after the sequon's Ser/Thr also reduces glycosylation (Gavel & von Heijne 1990); it is reported with the site rather than excluding it
  • does not predict aggregation, viscosity, immunogenicity or specificity

References

  • Bause E. Structural requirements of N-glycosylation of proteins. Studies with proline peptides as conformational probes. Biochem J. 1983;209(2):331-336. doi.org/10.1042/bj2090331
  • Gavel Y, von Heijne G. Sequence differences between glycosylated and non-glycosylated Asn-X-Thr/Ser acceptor sites: implications for protein engineering. Protein Eng. 1990;3(5):433-442. doi.org/10.1093/protein/3.5.433
  • Robinson NE, Robinson AB. Molecular clocks. Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A. 2001;98(3):944-949. doi.org/10.1073/pnas.98.3.944
  • Lu X, Nobrega RP, Lynaugh H, et al. Deamidation and isomerization liability analysis of 131 clinical-stage antibodies. MAbs. 2019;11(1):45-57. doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2018.1548233
  • Pan H, Chen K, Chu L, Kinderman F, Apostol I, Huang G. Methionine oxidation in human IgG2 Fc decreases binding affinities to protein A and FcRn. Protein Sci. 2009;18(2):424-433. doi.org/10.1002/pro.45
  • Vlasak J, Ionescu R. Fragmentation of monoclonal antibodies. MAbs. 2011;3(3):253-263. doi.org/10.4161/mabs.3.3.15608
  • Liu YD, Goetze AA, Bass RB, Flynn GC. N-terminal glutamate to pyroglutamate conversion in vivo for human IgG2 antibodies. J Biol Chem. 2011;286(13):11211-11217. doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M110.185041
  • Dick LW Jr, Qiu D, Mahon D, Adamo M, Cheng KC. C-terminal lysine variants in fully human monoclonal antibodies: investigation of test methods and possible causes. Biotechnol Bioeng. 2008;100(6):1132-1143. doi.org/10.1002/bit.21855

biologic.target_identity · version 1.0.0

Amino-acid identity of a target's orthologue to the human protein, by global Needleman-Wunsch alignment scored with BLOSUM62 and BLASTP gap penalties

Assumptions

  • the orthologue is the one NCBI Gene asserts for that species
  • identity is counted over all aligned columns, so a partial or predicted sequence scores lower rather than appearing identical over a fragment
  • terminal gaps are not penalised

Limits

  • identity is not binding and binding is not pharmacological activity; a conformational epitope can be lost between near-identical orthologues
  • many non-human primate RefSeq proteins are computationally predicted (XP_ accessions) rather than curated
  • says nothing about target expression, tissue distribution or downstream biology in that species

References

  • Needleman SB, Wunsch CD. A general method applicable to the search for similarities in the amino acid sequence of two proteins. J Mol Biol. 1970;48(3):443-453. doi.org/10.1016/0022-2836(70)90057-4
  • Henikoff S, Henikoff JG. Amino acid substitution matrices from protein blocks. Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A. 1992;89(22):10915-10919. doi.org/10.1073/pnas.89.22.10915
  • National Center for Biotechnology Information. NCBI Gene orthology and the NCBI Datasets API. National Library of Medicine. www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/datasets/docs/v2/
  • Cock PJA, Antao T, Chang JT, et al. Biopython: freely available Python tools for computational molecular biology and bioinformatics. Bioinformatics. 2009;25(11):1422-1423. doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btp163
