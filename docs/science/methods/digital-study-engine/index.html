digital.ames_qsar · version 1.0.0

Bacterial reverse-mutation (Ames) prediction by expert DNA-reactivity alerts (ICH M7 methodology 1)

Assumptions

  • a structure carrying a well-characterised DNA-reactive alert is predicted positive for bacterial mutagenicity; a structure carrying none, and within the domain, is predicted negative

Limits

  • an expert rule base (ICH M7 methodology 1); a complete assessment combines it with a statistical (Q)SAR (methodology 2) and expert review
  • detoxifying features and metabolism are not modelled; a positive alert is a flag for confirmation, not a verdict
  • alerts cover the common DNA-reactive classes, not every possible mechanism

References

  • ICH M7(R2): Assessment and control of DNA reactive (mutagenic) impurities in pharmaceuticals to limit potential carcinogenic risk. Step 4, 2023. (Two complementary (Q)SAR methodologies - one expert rule-based, one statistical - with expert review; a negative prediction from both supports a non-mutagenic conclusion.) database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_M7%28R2%29_Guideline_Step4_2023_0216.pdf
  • Benigni R, Bossa C. Structure alerts for carcinogenicity, and the Salmonella assay system: a novel insight through the chemical relational databases. Mutat Res. 2008;659(3):248-261. (Structural alerts for DNA-reactive mutagenicity used in the Toxtree / ISS rule base.) doi.org/10.1016/j.mrrev.2008.05.003
  • Ashby J, Tennant RW. Chemical structure, Salmonella mutagenicity and extent of carcinogenicity as indicators of genotoxic carcinogenesis among 222 chemicals tested in rodents by the U.S. NCI/NTP. Mutat Res. 1988;204(1):17-115. (The 'structural alerts' concept for DNA reactivity.) doi.org/10.1016/0165-1218(88)90114-0
  • OECD. Guidance Document on the Validation of (Quantitative) Structure-Activity Relationship [(Q)SAR] Models. OECD Series on Testing and Assessment No. 69, ENV/JM/MONO(2007)2, 2007. (The five OECD principles for QSAR validation: defined endpoint; unambiguous algorithm; defined domain of applicability; appropriate measures of goodness-of-fit, robustness and predictivity; mechanistic interpretation where possible.) www.oecd.org/env/guidance-document-on-the-validation-of-quantitative-structure-activity-relationship-q-sar-models-9789264085442-en.htm

digital.study_run · version 1.0.0

Digital preclinical study run across the derived study set with NAM credibility

Assumptions

  • each required study is answered by the most qualified in silico method available, with a credibility case graded for the context of use

Limits

  • every result is a model prediction, never a measurement; it informs a decision and never replaces a signed GxP study record on its own
  • where no quantitative model is qualified, the study is answered by a cited weight-of-evidence read with a targeted confirmatory recommendation

References

  • U.S. Food and Drug Administration. Roadmap to Reducing Animal Testing in Preclinical Safety Studies. April 2025. (Phased adoption of New Approach Methodologies - computational modelling, organ-on-chip and human-relevant in vitro assays - beginning with monoclonal antibodies.) www.fda.gov/media/186092/download
  • ASME V&V 40-2018. Assessing Credibility of Computational Modeling through Verification and Validation: Application to Medical Devices. ASME, 2018.
  • ICH M15: General Principles for Model-Informed Drug Development. Step 4 final guideline, adopted 29 January 2026; adopted by FDA as final guidance June 2026. database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_M15_Step4_Final_Guideline_2026_0129.pdf

digital.value_of_information · version 2.0.0

Expected Value of Information for preclinical experiment prioritisation (EVPI/EVSI)

Assumptions

  • the first-in-human decision is a go/no-go choice under an opportunity-loss model in which a correct decision costs nothing
  • both losses are supplied by the programme; there is no default ratio
  • EVSI is computed at specificity 1 and reported as an upper bound: no cited per-study specificity exists, and for a study no worse than chance (specificity >= 1 - sensitivity) a less specific test is a garbling of a more specific one, so EVSI cannot rise as specificity falls (Blackwell 1953)
  • each liability's decision is treated independently, so the programme EVPI is the sum of per-liability EVPIs and an experiment's EVSI is the sum over what it probes

Limits

  • EVPI/EVSI are exact for the stated loss model and posteriors; they rank what is worth learning, they do not themselves establish safety
  • the independence decomposition is conservative: correlated liabilities are not double-counted but joint resolution value is not modelled
  • absolute values scale with the loss model; the ranking depends only on its ratio

References

  • Raiffa H, Schlaifer R. Applied Statistical Decision Theory. Division of Research, Harvard Business School, Boston, 1961. (Foundational treatment of the expected value of perfect and sample information.)
  • Felli JC, Hazen GB. Sensitivity analysis and the expected value of perfect information. Med Decis Making. 1998;18(1):95-109. doi.org/10.1177/0272989X9801800117
  • Claxton K. The irrelevance of inference: a decision-making approach to the stochastic evaluation of health care technologies. J Health Econ. 1999;18(3):341-364. doi.org/10.1016/S0167-6296(98)00039-3
  • Heath A, Manolopoulou I, Baio G. A review of methods for analysis of the expected value of information. Med Decis Making. 2017;37(7):747-758. doi.org/10.1177/0272989X17697692
  • Blackwell D. Equivalent comparisons of experiments. Ann Math Stat. 1953;24(2):265-272. doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177729032
