In silico triage methods.

8 methods in this area. Each lists the assumptions it makes and the limits outside which its result is not valid.

triage.class_record · version 1.0.0

Class precedent: organ x finding counts across approved labels of one pharmacologic class (21 CFR 201.57 sections 13 and 8.1; members from FDA SPL class indexing via RxClass, labels from DailyMed)

Assumptions

  • on-target toxicity recurs across drugs of one pharmacologic class, so what approved members showed in animals is a prior for a new member
  • class membership is the FDA's own label indexing (EPC or MoA) as RxClass serves it; only direct members of the named class are read
  • each ingredient is counted once, from its most recently published NDA/BLA single-substance label; a generic (ANDA) label is read only when no NDA/BLA label is listed, and is marked as such; combination products are not read
  • a finding is read from one label sentence; organ and finding labels are this method's reading of that sentence and are shown beside it

Limits

  • a count over quotations, not a model: no probability or accuracy is claimed
  • off-target and chemotype-specific toxicity is not a class property and is not covered
  • labels summarise; findings a sponsor judged minor may not be stated, so absence from the labels is not evidence of absence
  • a first-in-class target has no approved members and returns no precedent
  • exposure multiples are as stated by each label, relative to that drug's own human dose; they are not transferable as doses to a new compound

triage.kestrel_sequence · version 1.0.0

KESTREL: the kill-early study order. Each required study is an imperfect test of the liabilities it covers, with cited detection sensitivity; its kill probability is the noisy-OR chance of a decisive finding; studies are ordered by increasing cost (or time) per unit kill probability (the series-system ratio rule), subject to the ICH precedence between studies

Assumptions

  • a programme clears only if no disqualifying liability fires: a series system, for which the expected-cost-minimising order of INDEPENDENT tests is increasing cost/P_kill (Mitten 1960; Unluyurt 2004)
  • P_kill(study) = 1 - product over covered liabilities k of (1 - pi_k * se_k), with pi_k the liability posterior and se_k the study's cited detection sensitivity for k (a lower bound: it counts only true decisive findings, as a negative animal result is near-uninformative, Bailey 2013)
  • study cost and duration are USER_SUPPLIED per CRO; where absent, flagged defaults are used and the order is reported as provisional
  • ICH precedence (dose range-finding before pivotal repeat-dose; bioanalytical validation before toxicokinetics) is a forest/series-parallel order, scheduled by a precedence-feasible greedy on the ratio (exact for series-parallel precedence by decomposition; Sidney 1975)

Limits

  • decision support only: it never commissions, waives or reorders a study of record, and never enters a submission
  • the ratio rule is provably optimal only for INDEPENDENT tests; the ICH studies share latent liabilities (general-tox range-finding and the pivotal cover the same organ panel), so under that dependence the order is a well-motivated heuristic, not a proven optimum, and no accuracy is claimed
  • the order is only as good as the cited sensitivities and the liability posteriors; both abstain rather than guess, and a study whose liabilities all abstain is listed without a ratio
  • general precedence-constrained sequencing is NP-hard (Lawler 1978); the ICH constraint set is series-parallel, so the greedy is exact for it

References

  • Mitten LG. An analytic solution to the least cost testing sequence problem. J Ind Eng. 1960;11(1):17. For a series system of independent pass/fail tests, expected cost is minimised by testing in increasing order of cost/failure-probability.
  • Unluyurt T. Sequential testing of complex systems: a review. Discrete Appl Math. 2004;142(1-3):189-205. Reviews the ratio rule for series/parallel systems and the complexity of precedence-constrained sequencing. doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2003.09.008
  • Sidney JB. Decomposition algorithms for single-machine sequencing with precedence relations and deferral costs. Oper Res. 1975;23(2):283-298. For series-parallel precedence the ratio-optimal order is obtained by decomposition. doi.org/10.1287/opre.23.2.283
  • Lawler EL. Sequencing jobs to minimize total weighted completion time subject to precedence constraints. Ann Discrete Math. 1978;2:75-90. Minimizing weighted completion time under general precedence is NP-hard; series-parallel precedence is solved in polynomial time by decomposition. doi.org/10.1016/S0167-5060(08)70323-6
  • Olson H, Betton G, Robinson D, et al. Concordance of the toxicity of pharmaceuticals in humans and in animals. Regul Toxicol Pharmacol. 2000;32(1):56-67. 150 compounds, 221 human toxicities; true-positive concordance 71% overall, non-rodent 63%, rodent 43%; per-system Table 3. doi.org/10.1006/rtph.2000.1399

triage.liability_graph · version 1.0.0

Posterior probability of a per-organ/mechanism safety liability, pooled in log-odds from the triage evidence the system already holds: class-precedent rates (Beta-Binomial over approved-label counts), cited physicochemical odds ratios, read-across/measured hERG potency, and cited modality class-effects

Assumptions

  • liabilities are organ/mechanism nodes; each cited piece of evidence shifts a node's log-odds by a term whose weight comes from a cited odds or likelihood ratio, or from the Beta-Binomial posterior of a class-precedence rate
  • the base rate is a weakly-informative prior from candidate-attrition data (Roberts 2014), not a measurement, and is overridable
  • a class rate at or below the base rate carries no positive evidence and contributes no term; the base rate alone never produces a posterior
  • terms are pooled additively (sum of log-likelihood-ratios); they are not assumed independent, so where several load one node the posterior can over-concentrate and is read as an ordinal prior, not a calibrated probability

Limits

  • decision support only: a liability posterior is never evidence, never a study record and never part of a submission (docs/04 section 4.26)
  • a node with no cited evidence term is omitted (abstains) rather than reporting a base-rate-only number
  • an expert-set term (hERG/potency prior, a modality class-effect weight) is marked as requiring verification and is not a number stated by its citation
  • no sensitivity, specificity or calibration is claimed until a pre-registered, held-out validation is published

References

triage.modality_class_effects · version 1.0.0

Recurrent nonclinical liabilities expected for a drug modality, from cited toxicologic-pathology reviews (e.g. ASO complement and thrombocytopenia; GalNAc-siRNA hepatic; AAV hepatic and dorsal-root-ganglion; ADC payload classes; T-cell-engager cytokine release)

Assumptions

  • a modality's mechanism and chemistry recur across its members, so published class effects are a prior for a new member of that modality

Limits

  • decision support only; a modality with no cited class-effect in the table contributes nothing rather than a guess
  • off-target and chemotype-specific toxicity is not covered by a modality rule

triage.observation_forecast · version 1.0.0

Ranked forecast of the organ-system observations a repeat-dose study is most likely to report, assembled per modality from class precedent (on-target recurrence), cited physicochemical rules (off-target) and cited modality class-effects; each row carries its evidence and claims no accuracy

Assumptions

  • a finding is forecast only where a cited evidence source places it: a class member's label, a physicochemical rule, or a modality class-effect review
  • likelihood is reported as an ordinal band (watch / likely / expected), not a calibrated probability

Limits

  • decision support only; abstains per organ where no cited evidence exists
  • forecasts where a finding may appear, not whether it will; no accuracy is claimed until a pre-registered, held-out validation is published

triage.pad_reverse_dosimetry · version 1.0.0

Pharmacologically active dose by steady-state reverse dosimetry: daily dose = (EC50 x MW / fu) x CL x 24 h / F

Assumptions

  • average steady-state concentration Css = F x dose rate / CL (linear pharmacokinetics)
  • the unbound plasma concentration is the concentration at the target (free-drug hypothesis)
  • the species' target has the potency measured in the assay
  • where the record holds several values, the one giving the lower dose is used (highest fu, lowest intravenous clearance, highest F, most potent primary assay)

Limits

  • estimates where on-target pharmacology begins; it is not a NOAEL and predicts no toxicity
  • applicability: inside the domain only when the potency is interpolated, clearance is measured in vivo, and the target concentration lies within the exposures measured; otherwise the value is flagged out of domain
  • no accuracy is claimed until a pre-registered, held-out validation is published

References

  • U.S. Food and Drug Administration. Guidance for Industry: Estimating the Maximum Safe Starting Dose in Initial Clinical Trials for Therapeutics in Adult Healthy Volunteers. CDER, July 2005. www.fda.gov/media/72309/download
  • Wetmore BA, Wambaugh JF, Ferguson SS, et al. Integration of dosimetry, exposure, and high-throughput screening data in chemical toxicity assessment. Toxicol Sci. 2012;125(1):157-174. doi.org/10.1093/toxsci/kfr254
  • Smith DA, Di L, Kerns EH. The effect of plasma protein binding on in vivo efficacy: misconceptions in drug discovery. Nat Rev Drug Discov. 2010;9(12):929-939. doi.org/10.1038/nrd3287

triage.read_across · version 2.1.0

Similarity-weighted read-across over measured analogue values (GenRA-style)

Assumptions

  • structurally similar compounds have similar measured values for the endpoint
  • analogue values are weighted by ECFP4 Tanimoto similarity to the query, raised to the method's weighting power so the nearest analogues dominate
  • potency values are averaged on the logarithmic (pIC50) scale

Limits

  • requires a stated minimum number of analogues above the similarity threshold; otherwise the value is UNAVAILABLE and nothing is predicted. The threshold and count were set on a held-out fold against a bar fixed beforehand; how often the method can answer at all is bounded by how many compounds have been measured for the endpoint
  • censored values (reported as a bound) are excluded, so a compound with only bounded measurements contributes no analogue value
  • the analogue set reflects the external database at the time of retrieval; the analogues used are recorded with the result
  • averaging shrinks an estimate toward the mean of its neighbourhood, so the point estimate under-states the potency of potent compounds; a screening decision is taken on the endpoint's calibrated interval instead
  • no accuracy is claimed until a pre-registered, held-out validation is published for the endpoint

References

  • Shah I, Liu J, Judson RS, Thomas RS, Patlewicz G. Systematically evaluating read-across prediction and performance using a local validity approach characterized by chemical structure and bioactivity information. Regul Toxicol Pharmacol. 2016;79:12-24. doi.org/10.1016/j.yrtph.2016.05.008
  • Helman G, Shah I, Patlewicz G. Transitioning the generalised read-across approach (GenRA) to quantitative predictions: a case study using acute oral toxicity data. Comput Toxicol. 2019;12:100097. doi.org/10.1016/j.comtox.2019.100097
  • Rogers D, Hahn M. Extended-connectivity fingerprints. J Chem Inf Model. 2010;50(5):742-754. doi.org/10.1021/ci100050t

triage.structural_alerts · version 1.0.0

Substructure screen against a published structural-alert set (RDKit FilterCatalog)

Assumptions

  • an alert is a substructure match in the published set, not a measurement and not a prediction of any in vivo outcome

Limits

  • alert sets differ in purpose and were derived for screening-library triage, not for regulatory hazard classification
  • a structure with no alert is not thereby shown to be safe: the sets cover only the functionality their authors catalogued
  • no sensitivity or specificity is claimed for any endpoint
