Methods registry

Virtual-study projection methods.

8 methods in this area. Each lists the assumptions it makes and the limits outside which its result is not valid.

virtual.critical_exposure_interval · version 1.0.0

Interval-censored critical effect exposure from a study's NOAEL and LOAEL

Assumptions

  • the true threshold for the endpoint lies above the exposure at the NOAEL and at or below the exposure at the lowest adverse dose

Limits

  • a NOAEL is bounded by the dose spacing of the study that produced it; the interval, not a point, is the inferential object
  • an interval is open above when the top dose produced no adverse effect

References

  • U.S. Environmental Protection Agency. Benchmark Dose Technical Guidance. EPA/100/R-12/001, June 2012. (A NOAEL is bounded by the spacing of the dose groups; benchmark-dose modelling estimates the response level directly.) www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-01/documents/benchmark_dose_guidance.pdf
  • Slob W. Dose-response modeling of continuous endpoints. Toxicol Sci. 2002;66(2):298-312. doi.org/10.1093/toxsci/66.2.298

virtual.duration_extrapolation · version 1.0.0

Duration extrapolation of a critical effect level by default assessment factors

Assumptions

  • default factors: 14 to 28 days 1.5, 28 to 90 days 3, 90 days to chronic 2

Limits

  • defaults are regulatory conventions, not compound-specific measurements
  • no factor is applied for shortening a duration; the shorter study's level is carried unchanged and flagged

References

  • European Chemicals Agency. Guidance on information requirements and chemical safety assessment, Chapter R.8: Characterisation of dose [concentration]-response for human health. Version 2.1, November 2012. (Default duration extrapolation assessment factors: subacute to subchronic 3, subchronic to chronic 2.) echa.europa.eu/documents/10162/13632/information_requirements_r8_en.pdf

virtual.exposure_allometric · version 1.0.0

Interspecies exposure projection by allometric clearance scaling

Assumptions

  • clearance scales with body weight to the power 0.75

Limits

  • used only when observed toxicokinetics are absent in the target species
  • observed fold-error against measured values is reported with every projection

References

  • Mahmood I, Balian JD. The pharmacokinetic principles behind scaling from preclinical results to phase I protocols. Clin Pharmacokinet. 1999;36(1):1-11. doi.org/10.2165/00003088-199936010-00001
  • U.S. Food and Drug Administration. Guidance for Industry: Estimating the Maximum Safe Starting Dose in Initial Clinical Trials for Therapeutics in Adult Healthy Volunteers. CDER, July 2005. www.fda.gov/media/72309/download

virtual.exposure_from_dose · version 1.0.0

Systemic exposure at a dose from observed dose-normalised toxicokinetics

Assumptions

  • exposure is proportional to dose over the interpolated range
  • the dose-normalised value is taken from toxicokinetics in the same study design (species, strain, route, vehicle) wherever one exists

Limits

  • linearity is checked against the observed dose levels and the value is refused outside the observed range by more than fivefold
  • saturable metabolite formation (for example ALA to PpIX) is not linear and is handled by its own metabolite relation, never by this one

References

  • Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency (Japan). Review Report: Alaglio Oral 1.5 g (aminolevulinic acid hydrochloride). 25 January 2013. (Rat 4-week and 13-week and dog 4-week oral toxicity studies with toxicokinetics.) www.pmda.go.jp/files/000153482.pdf

virtual.exposure_from_reported_ratio · version 1.0.0

Absolute exposure reconstructed from a reported dose-ratio and an anchor value

Assumptions

  • the ratio and the anchor come from the same study population and assay

Limits

  • the reconstructed value inherits the precision of the reported ratio, which is usually given to two significant figures

References

  • U.S. Food and Drug Administration. Clinical Pharmacology and Biopharmaceutics Review, NDA 208630 (Gleolan). 2017. (Dose-ranging study MC-ALS.8-I: relative to 0.2 mg/kg, AUC rose 6.2-fold at 2 mg/kg and 49.9-fold at 20 mg/kg.) www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2017/208630Orig1s000ClinPharmR.pdf

virtual.ppix_accumulation · version 1.0.0

Protoporphyrin IX accumulation from exogenous 5-aminolevulinic acid

Assumptions

  • exogenous ALA bypasses haem feedback inhibition of ALA synthase so PpIX accumulates where ferrochelatase capacity is exceeded

Limits

  • PpIX formation is saturable: a tenfold ALA dose raised human PpIX exposure about threefold, so PpIX is never scaled linearly with ALA dose
  • tissue PpIX is not predicted; only the plasma relation that was measured

References

  • Wachowska M, Muchowicz A, Firczuk M, et al. Aminolevulinic acid (ALA) as a prodrug in photodynamic therapy of cancer. Molecules. 2011;16(5):4140-4164. doi.org/10.3390/molecules16054140
  • Yang X, Palasuberniam P, Kraus D, Chen B. Aminolevulinic acid-based tumor detection and therapy: molecular mechanisms and strategies for enhancement. Int J Mol Sci. 2015;16(10):25865-25880. doi.org/10.3390/ijms161025865

virtual.species_projection · version 1.0.0

Cross-species projection of an endpoint's critical exposure

Assumptions

  • for an endpoint with no cited species difference, the critical systemic exposure is conserved across species
  • an endpoint that a species physiologically cannot express is excluded for that species rather than projected into it

Limits

  • exposure conservation is an assumption, not a law; its observed fold-error on the programme's own validation set is reported with every projection
  • a species-specific sensitivity factor is applied only where a cited mechanism supports it

References

  • ICH Harmonised Tripartite Guideline M3(R2): Guidance on Nonclinical Safety Studies for the Conduct of Human Clinical Trials and Marketing Authorization for Pharmaceuticals. Step 4, 11 June 2009. database.ich.org/sites/default/files/M3_R2__Guideline.pdf
  • Horn CC, Kimball BA, Wang H, et al. Why can't rodents vomit? A comparative behavioral, anatomical, and physiological study. PLoS One. 2013;8(4):e60537. doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0060537

virtual.study_projection · version 1.0.0

Projected findings and NOAEL of a proposed repeat-dose study

Assumptions

  • the study reports an adverse finding at a dose whose projected exposure reaches the endpoint's critical exposure interval

Limits

  • a projection of what a study would report; it is not the study and cannot substitute for it in a regulatory submission
  • endpoints with no cited evidence in the programme are not projected

References

  • ICH Harmonised Tripartite Guideline M3(R2): Guidance on Nonclinical Safety Studies for the Conduct of Human Clinical Trials and Marketing Authorization for Pharmaceuticals. Step 4, 11 June 2009. database.ich.org/sites/default/files/M3_R2__Guideline.pdf

(A NOAEL is bounded by the spacing of the dose groups; benchmark-dose modelling estimates the response level directly.) www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-01/documents/benchmark_dose_guidance.pdf
