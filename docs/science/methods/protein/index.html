protein.a280_01_percent · version 1.0.0

Absorbance at 280 nm of a 1 g/L solution through 1 cm: extinction coefficient divided by molecular mass

Assumptions

  • the mass used is the polypeptide mass of the same chain

Limits

  • a glycosylated product weighs more than its polypeptide, so its measured A280 per mg/mL is lower than this value

References

  • Pace CN, Vajdos F, Fee L, Grimsley G, Gray T. How to measure and predict the molar absorption coefficient of a protein. Protein Sci. 1995;4(11):2411-2423. doi.org/10.1002/pro.5560041120
  • Gasteiger E, Hoogland C, Gattiker A, Duvaud S, Wilkins MR, Appel RD, Bairoch A. Protein identification and analysis tools on the ExPASy server. In: Walker JM, ed. The Proteomics Protocols Handbook. Humana Press; 2005:571-607. doi.org/10.1385/1-59259-890-0:571

protein.aliphatic_index · version 1.0.0

Aliphatic index: relative volume of aliphatic side chains (Ikai 1980)

Assumptions

  • mole percent of Ala, Val, Ile and Leu, with the coefficients 2.9 and 3.9 as ExPASy ProtParam states Ikai's index; the 1980 paper defines the index in words and the coefficients are taken from ProtParam's statement of it

Limits

  • correlated with thermostability across globular proteins; it is not a melting temperature and does not apply to a specific formulation
  • implementations differ over mole percent versus raw counts; this one uses mole percent, so an all-alanine chain scores exactly 100

References

  • Ikai A. Thermostability and aliphatic index of globular proteins. J Biochem. 1980;88(6):1895-1898. doi.org/10.1093/oxfordjournals.jbchem.a133168
  • Gasteiger E, Hoogland C, Gattiker A, Duvaud S, Wilkins MR, Appel RD, Bairoch A. Protein identification and analysis tools on the ExPASy server. In: Walker JM, ed. The Proteomics Protocols Handbook. Humana Press; 2005:571-607. doi.org/10.1385/1-59259-890-0:571

protein.extinction_280 · version 1.0.0

Molar absorption coefficient at 280 nm in water from tryptophan, tyrosine and cystine content (Pace et al. 1995)

Assumptions

  • absorbance in water at 280 nm
  • the number of disulfide bonds is supplied by the caller; the sequence does not say which cysteines are paired

Limits

  • against 116 measured values the paper reports a mean deviation of 3.2 per cent for proteins containing tryptophan and 6.5 per cent for those without it, with individual errors above 10 per cent among the latter
  • the paper recommends measuring the coefficient rather than predicting it where the value matters
  • non-protein chromophores, including conjugated payloads, are not counted

References

  • Pace CN, Vajdos F, Fee L, Grimsley G, Gray T. How to measure and predict the molar absorption coefficient of a protein. Protein Sci. 1995;4(11):2411-2423. doi.org/10.1002/pro.5560041120
  • Gasteiger E, Hoogland C, Gattiker A, Duvaud S, Wilkins MR, Appel RD, Bairoch A. Protein identification and analysis tools on the ExPASy server. In: Walker JM, ed. The Proteomics Protocols Handbook. Humana Press; 2005:571-607. doi.org/10.1385/1-59259-890-0:571
  • Cock PJA, Antao T, Chang JT, et al. Biopython: freely available Python tools for computational molecular biology and bioinformatics. Bioinformatics. 2009;25(11):1422-1423. doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btp163

protein.gravy · version 1.0.0

Grand average of hydropathy: mean Kyte-Doolittle hydropathy per residue

Assumptions

None stated.

Limits

  • a composition average; it does not locate hydrophobic patches, which is what aggregation and non-specific clearance actually track
  • defined only for the 20 standard residues

References

  • Kyte J, Doolittle RF. A simple method for displaying the hydropathic character of a protein. J Mol Biol. 1982;157(1):105-132. doi.org/10.1016/0022-2836(82)90515-0
  • Cock PJA, Antao T, Chang JT, et al. Biopython: freely available Python tools for computational molecular biology and bioinformatics. Bioinformatics. 2009;25(11):1422-1423. doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btp163

protein.instability_index · version 1.0.0

Instability index from dipeptide composition (Guruprasad et al. 1990)

Assumptions

  • the dipeptide instability weights were fitted to 12 unstable and 32 stable proteins classified by their in vivo (metabolic) stability

Limits

  • the cut-off of 40 below which a protein is called stable is ExPASy ProtParam's statement of the method, not a figure quoted here from the 1990 paper, and it is not a specification limit for a biologic drug substance
  • the index was built for intracellular stability, not for the stability of a purified protein in a formulation, which is what matters to a drug product
  • no information about the formulation, which dominates real stability

References

  • Guruprasad K, Reddy BVB, Pandit MW. Correlation between stability of a protein and its dipeptide composition: a novel approach for predicting in vivo stability of a protein from its primary sequence. Protein Eng. 1990;4(2):155-161. doi.org/10.1093/protein/4.2.155
  • Gasteiger E, Hoogland C, Gattiker A, Duvaud S, Wilkins MR, Appel RD, Bairoch A. Protein identification and analysis tools on the ExPASy server. In: Walker JM, ed. The Proteomics Protocols Handbook. Humana Press; 2005:571-607. doi.org/10.1385/1-59259-890-0:571

protein.isoelectric_point · version 1.0.0

Theoretical isoelectric point from the amino-acid sequence (Bjellqvist pK set, as implemented by Biopython ProtParam)

Assumptions

  • free, unmodified N- and C-termini
  • charge of the unfolded chain; the folded structure shifts individual pK values

Limits

  • post-translational modification, bound counter-ions and the formulation buffer are not represented
  • defined only for the 20 standard residues; a sequence containing an ambiguity code has no value

References

  • Bjellqvist B, Hughes GJ, Pasquali C, et al. The focusing positions of polypeptides in immobilized pH gradients can be predicted from their amino acid sequences. Electrophoresis. 1993;14(10):1023-1031. doi.org/10.1002/elps.11501401163
  • Bjellqvist B, Basse B, Olsen E, Celis JE. Reference points for comparisons of two-dimensional maps of proteins from different human cell types defined in a pH scale where isoelectric points correlate with polypeptide compositions. Electrophoresis. 1994;15(3-4):529-539. doi.org/10.1002/elps.1150150171

protein.mass_disulfide_corrected · version 1.0.0

Average polypeptide mass less two hydrogen atoms per declared disulfide bond

Assumptions

  • the number of disulfide bonds is declared by the sponsor

Limits

  • glycans and other post-translational modification are not included, so for a glycoprotein this is the polypeptide mass and not the product's mass

References

  • SIB Swiss Institute of Bioinformatics, ExPASy. Protein Identification Tools - Mass values used (FindMod): average and monoisotopic amino-acid residue masses and water. web.expasy.org/findmod/findmod_masses.html
  • Prohaska T, Irrgeher J, Benefield J, et al. Standard atomic weights of the elements 2021 (IUPAC Technical Report). Pure Appl Chem. 2022;94(5):573-600. doi.org/10.1515/pac-2019-0603

protein.net_charge · version 1.0.0

Net charge of a polypeptide at a stated pH (Bjellqvist pK set, Biopython ProtParam)

Assumptions

  • free, unmodified termini; charge of the unfolded chain

Limits

  • ignores the folded structure, ion pairing and the formulation buffer
  • defined only for the 20 standard residues
