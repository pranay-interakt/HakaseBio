NAM credibility (ASME V&V40) methods.

3 methods in this area. Each lists the assumptions it makes and the limits outside which its result is not valid.

nam.applicability_domain · version 1.0.0

Applicability-domain determination for a model prediction (OECD principle 3)

Assumptions

  • a prediction is trustworthy only for substances within the chemical and biological space the model was built and validated on

Limits

  • a substance outside the domain is flagged out-of-domain and its prediction is carried as hypothesis-generating, not as a quantified result

References

  • OECD. Guidance Document on the Validation of (Quantitative) Structure-Activity Relationship [(Q)SAR] Models. OECD Series on Testing and Assessment No. 69, ENV/JM/MONO(2007)2, 2007. (The five OECD principles for QSAR validation: defined endpoint; unambiguous algorithm; defined domain of applicability; appropriate measures of goodness-of-fit, robustness and predictivity; mechanistic interpretation where possible.) www.oecd.org/env/guidance-document-on-the-validation-of-quantitative-structure-activity-relationship-q-sar-models-9789264085442-en.htm

nam.credibility_assessment · version 2.0.0

Risk-informed credibility gradation of a computational model result (ASME V&V40)

Assumptions

  • each of verification, validation, applicability and uncertainty quantification is graded on V&V40's ordinal gradation of rigor (low / medium / high) with a written basis
  • the rigor every factor must reach equals the model-risk level (V&V40: rigor commensurate with model risk); no numeric index, weighting or cut-off is used
  • tier rule: adequate when every factor meets the required rigor; exploratory when validation or applicability falls short (OECD (Q)SAR principles 3-4); supportive otherwise

Limits

  • a credibility tier records whether the assembled evidence meets the goal for the stated context of use; it is not a probability that the prediction is correct
  • it never upgrades a prediction to a measurement, and never authorises a signed GxP record on its own

References

  • ASME V&V 40-2018. Assessing Credibility of Computational Modeling through Verification and Validation: Application to Medical Devices. ASME, 2018.
  • U.S. Food and Drug Administration. Assessing the Credibility of Computational Modeling and Simulation in Medical Device Submissions: Guidance for Industry and FDA Staff. Finalised November 2023. (Adapts the ASME V&V40 risk-informed credibility framework for regulatory submissions.) www.fda.gov/media/154985/download
  • ICH M15: General Principles for Model-Informed Drug Development. Step 4 final guideline, adopted 29 January 2026; adopted by FDA as final guidance June 2026. database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_M15_Step4_Final_Guideline_2026_0129.pdf

nam.model_risk · version 2.0.0

Model risk from model influence and decision consequence (ASME V&V40)

Assumptions

  • model risk rises with how much the decision relies on the model (influence) and with the harm of an incorrect decision (consequence)
  • the level is read from the FDA 2025 risk matrix (Fig. 1), shared with hos.validation.credibility so the platform grades risk one way

Limits

  • risk is a gradation to size the credibility evidence, not a probability
  • influence and consequence are assigned for a stated context of use and do not transfer to a different decision

References

  • ASME V&V 40-2018. Assessing Credibility of Computational Modeling through Verification and Validation: Application to Medical Devices. ASME, 2018.
  • U.S. Food and Drug Administration. Assessing the Credibility of Computational Modeling and Simulation in Medical Device Submissions: Guidance for Industry and FDA Staff. Finalised November 2023. (Adapts the ASME V&V40 risk-informed credibility framework for regulatory submissions.) www.fda.gov/media/154985/download
