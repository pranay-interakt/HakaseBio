Methods registry

ADME methods.

8 methods in this area. Each lists the assumptions it makes and the limits outside which its result is not valid.

adme.clh_well_stirred · version 1.0.0

Hepatic clearance by the well-stirred (venous equilibrium) model

Assumptions

  • the liver is a single well-mixed compartment
  • only unbound drug is available for metabolism
  • hepatic metabolism is the dominant clearance route

Limits

  • ignores permeability limitation and transporter-mediated uptake
  • population-mean blood flow and binding; no inter-individual variability

References

  • Wilkinson GR, Shand DG. Commentary: a physiological approach to hepatic drug clearance. Clin Pharmacol Ther. 1975;18(4):377-390. doi.org/10.1002/cpt1975184377
  • Obach RS. Prediction of human clearance of twenty-nine drugs from hepatic microsomal intrinsic clearance data: an examination of in vitro half-life approach and nonspecific binding to microsomes. Drug Metab Dispos. 1999;27(11):1350-1359. doi.org/10.1016/S0090-9556(24)14938-0

adme.clint_invitro · version 1.0.0

Apparent in vitro intrinsic clearance from depletion half-life

Assumptions

  • first-order depletion
  • no significant non-specific binding unless corrected

Limits

  • scaled per mg microsomal protein; hepatocyte data require a different scalar

References

  • Obach RS. Prediction of human clearance of twenty-nine drugs from hepatic microsomal intrinsic clearance data: an examination of in vitro half-life approach and nonspecific binding to microsomes. Drug Metab Dispos. 1999;27(11):1350-1359. doi.org/10.1016/S0090-9556(24)14938-0
  • Houston JB. Utility of in vitro drug metabolism data in predicting in vivo metabolic clearance. Biochem Pharmacol. 1994;47(9):1469-1479. doi.org/10.1016/0006-2952(94)90520-7

adme.depletion_fit · version 1.0.0

First-order substrate depletion fit (ln-linear regression)

Assumptions

  • substrate concentration is well below Km so depletion is first-order
  • only the linear region of ln(fraction remaining) vs time is fitted

Limits

  • an R-squared below the configured threshold, or total depletion under about 20%, makes the rate constant poorly estimable

References

  • Obach RS. Prediction of human clearance of twenty-nine drugs from hepatic microsomal intrinsic clearance data: an examination of in vitro half-life approach and nonspecific binding to microsomes. Drug Metab Dispos. 1999;27(11):1350-1359. doi.org/10.1016/S0090-9556(24)14938-0

adme.fu_mic_predicted · version 1.0.0

Predicted microsomal unbound fraction from lipophilicity

Assumptions

  • binding is driven by lipophilicity at the stated microsomal concentration

Limits

  • reliable for logP/D 0-3 (below 5 at <= 0.1 mg/mL protein) per Gertz 2008; outside that range fu,mic should be measured

References

  • Hallifax D, Houston JB. Binding of drugs to hepatic microsomes: comment and assessment of current prediction methodology with recommendation for improvement. Drug Metab Dispos. 2006;34(4):724-726. doi.org/10.1124/dmd.105.007658
  • Gertz M, Kilford PJ, Houston JB, Galetin A. Drug lipophilicity and microsomal protein concentration as determinants in the prediction of the fraction unbound in microsomal incubations. Drug Metab Dispos. 2008;36(3):535-542. doi.org/10.1124/dmd.107.018713

adme.percent_remaining · version 1.0.0

Parent remaining relative to the zero-time sample

Assumptions

  • response is proportional to concentration over the measured range

Limits

  • a ratio of instrument responses; requires a common internal standard

References
