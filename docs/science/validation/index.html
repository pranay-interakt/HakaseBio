Science · Validation

Tested on drugs with known answers.

We validate BioSim the way a reviewer would: on approved drugs whose answers are known, with each drug’s own data held out. Results are published here in full, including where the system falls short.

End-to-end run

40 approved drugs, run as if they were new.

40 approved small-molecule drugs (9 since withdrawn) were each registered by structure alone and run through the whole pipeline. Every measurement of the drug itself was removed from the screening reference set first, so each prediction was made without the answer. The drug's own measured hERG potency is the ground truth.

  • Runs completed without error: 40/40. The provenance ledger reconciled after every run: 40/40. Values returned without provenance: 0.
  • hERG potency by read-across answered for 15/40 drugs and declined 25. Declining is the designed behaviour when fewer than two close, measured analogues exist.
  • When it answered, the median fold error was 8.8x (8/15 within 10-fold); the stated interval contained the measured value in 8/15 cases.
  • As a screen for potent blockers (IC50 at or below 1,000 nM, judged on the interval’s lower end), sensitivity was 90% (9/10 truly potent drugs caught) and specificity 0% (0/5). Missed: Olanzapine. Over-called: Bedaquiline, Chlorpromazine, Clarithromycin, Fexofenadine, Fluoxetine.

Read-across is decision support for choosing and designing studies. It never replaces the hERG assay that ICH S7B requires, and it never enters a submission.

Model card

hERG potency by read-across.

Question. Before a hERG assay is commissioned, is this compound likely to block the hERG (Kv11.1) channel, and at roughly what potency?

Role. Decision support for study selection and design only. It never becomes a study record, a readiness count or part of a submission, and it never substitutes for the in vitro hERG assay required by ICH S7B.

Validation. Tested on 2,263 held-out compounds from a public cardiac ion-channel benchmark. It answered for 756 and declined the rest; for calling potent blockers, AUROC was 0.82, sensitivity 0.83 and specificity 0.72.

Full statistics

Validated on 2263 held-out compounds from CardioSafe multi-channel cardiac ion-channel benchmark (v1 labels, tan70 split) (19266 reference, 812 calibration), source https://github.com/AppliedScientific/CardioSafe-benchmark. Applicability-domain coverage: predicted 756 of 2263 (33.4%); the rest declined (too few close analogues). External predictivity (in-domain): RMSE 0.6787 (95% CI 0.6163 to 0.742) pIC50, MAE 0.4437, R² 0.381 (95% CI 0.2719 to 0.4697), Spearman 0.6462. Golbraikh-Tropsha: R² 0.381, slope 0.9914, criterion -0.0073 — does not pass. Y-randomisation (labels shuffled): R² -0.7091, RMSE 1.1278 — predictivity collapses, so the signal is not chance correlation. Conformal prediction intervals: 80% interval ±0.6578 covers 76.2%, 90% interval ±0.9908 covers 88.5%. Downstream binary gate (blocker at the stated cut): sensitivity 0.832, specificity 0.7164, balanced accuracy 0.7742, MCC 0.5457, AUROC 0.8204.

Out of scope. Peptides, antibodies and other biologics; compounds with no near analogues; and any use as evidence of safety or of a safety margin
