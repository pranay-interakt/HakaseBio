Thesis

Sell the outcome. Own no labs. Keep the record.

  • What we sell

    Preclinical development as an outcome. A biotech brings a drug candidate and gets back the planned studies, the analysed results and the nonclinical package for its IND, instead of managing several labs by email.

  • How we deliver it

    A core that holds the study lifecycle, the audit trail and the human sign-offs, plus study drivers that each run one study type end to end with published methods.

  • Why it compounds

    We coordinate lab capacity and sell the outcome. Every programme adds structured, comparable data across labs to one record, an asset that grows with each programme.

What we built

Eight capabilities, built in-house.

From the planning rules to the verifier that checks the finished package, the parts of BioSim are our own. Each is working software today.

  • Regulatory engine The study plan, derived and cited

    Reads a programme’s facts and derives every study the ICH, FDA and WHO guidelines require, for nine drug types, each line cited to its section. An unknown fact leaves a study conditional, never assumed.

    More
  • KESTREL Kill-early study sequencing

    Orders the study battery so a programme-ending finding surfaces at the lowest expected spend: each study’s chance of a decisive result, from cited detection rates, against its cost and time, inside the order the guidelines set.

    More
  • SEXTANT The value of an experiment

    Prices each candidate study by how much it could change the go or no-go decision, using the programme’s own costs of a wrong go and a wrong stop. Experiments are ranked by value, and by value per unit cost.

    More
  • Digital study engine In silico answers, graded for credibility

    Answers each required study with the most qualified computational method available and grades the credibility of that answer for its context of use under ASME V&V 40. A prediction informs a decision; it never replaces a signed study.

    More
  • Study drivers One study type, end to end

    Fourteen study types, from microsomal stability to GLP repeat-dose toxicology, each packaged with its protocol, data checks, cited analysis and report, so every lab is held to the same standard.

    More
  • Provenance ledger Every value with its origin

    Eight ways a number can exist, none of them guessed. An append-only, hash-chained record of every change, with signatures only people can give, and simulated data that can never become evidence.

    More
  • Offline verifier The package checks itself

    Every Module 4 export carries what is needed to re-run each analysis from the raw files and check every fingerprint, ledger entry and signature, without BioSim, a database or a network.

    More
  • HAIOps Hakase’s discipline for regulated AI

    Six requirements every workflow is built to: traceability, quantified confidence, human authority over safety decisions, applicability monitoring, model governance and integrity of evidence.

    More

The problem

The path to a first human dose is slow and fragmented.

  • Fragmented

    A single programme runs studies across several contract labs. Each returns its own reports and data formats, and the sponsor stitches them together.

  • Manual

    Study plans, protocols and FDA datasets are assembled by hand. Errors often surface late, at submission.

  • Hard to defend

    Every figure in a submission has to trace back to the raw data behind it. Spreadsheets and PDFs make that chain hard to show.

Why now

The rules for reaching first-in-human are changing.

Each change rewards a plan that is cited, versioned and right the first time, and a record that can be checked.

  1. Apr 2025

    FDA publishes its roadmap to reduce animal testing in preclinical safety studies.1

  2. Mar 2026

    FDA publishes draft guidance on how new approach methodologies can be validated for use in drug development submissions.2

  3. Sep 2026

    FDA opens an expedited IND pilot for first-in-human trials, noting that US first-in-human studies can take up to two years.3

  4. Sep 2026

    A final rule writes “nonclinical” in place of “animal” across FDA’s regulations.4

Questions
Questions investors ask first.

  
Do you run labs?
No. BioSim works with contract labs and in-house teams. Owning animal facilities is capital-heavy and slow to scale; orchestrating them is not.
Does the AI make regulatory decisions?
No. Models draft, rank and check. A named person makes and signs every scientific decision. Simulated values never enter a submission, and any predicted value is labelled as predicted.
What is built today?
The study planning engine across nine drug types, study prioritisation, study drivers from lab assays to GLP toxicology, the signed audit trail, Module 4 assembly and SEND export. Validation against approved drugs is published on our science pages.
Why can’t a CRO or an incumbent do this?
A contract lab sells its own capacity, so it cannot credibly route work to competitors. Document tools stop at the paperwork. BioSim is lab-neutral and built around a traceable record from the first plan to the IND.
What is KESTREL?
Our kill-early study sequencer. It orders a programme’s studies so that, if the candidate is going to fail, it fails as early and as cheaply as possible, using cited detection rates and the programme’s own costs, inside the order the guidelines require. It is decision support; the sponsor’s team decides what to commission.
What is HAIOps?
Hakase’s operating discipline for AI in regulated healthcare work: traceability, quantified confidence, human authority over safety decisions, applicability monitoring, model governance and integrity of evidence. BioSim is built to it.
How do you make money?
Per drug programme, priced against the services budget a sponsor already has for preclinical work. A readiness review, the entry point, is priced per study reviewed.
