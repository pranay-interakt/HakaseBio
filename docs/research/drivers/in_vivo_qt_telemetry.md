## Which correction does the Q&A recommend?

**Individual QT correction derived from the animal's own QT–RR relationship**, in preference to the generic
Bazett / Fridericia / Van de Water formulae, *in certain cases* — specifically when the test drug affects
heart rate (S7B Q&A 3.3, quoted verbatim above). It is a preference with a stated justification, not an
absolute mandate: S7B §3.1.3 still allows generic formulae provided the choice is "justified with data from
the test system", and the Q&A requires the QTc-vs-RR independence evidence (plots, number of matched pairs,
correlation metric, 95 % CIs, p-values) whichever method is used. On the clinical side, E14 Q&A 1.5 (p.9)
states Bazett has been shown to be an inferior correction method in adults and that Fridericia is likely
appropriate in most situations — clinical wording, not nonclinical.

## Implications for the driver

- Correction method is a **required, recorded choice** with its justification artefact: QTc-vs-RR plot, number
  of matched QT–RR pairs, correlation metric, 95 % CI and p-value (S7B Q&A 3.3). Individual-animal correction
  must be available and should be the default offered when the drug changes HR; the reference cycle length
  (e.g. 750 ms, 500 ms, 75 bpm) is an explicit input, never a hard-coded constant, because published
  implementations differ.
- Bazett and Fridericia may be implemented as `QTcB = QT/√RR` and `QTcF = QT/RR^(1/3)` (RR in seconds) citing
  Vandenberk 2016 as the readable statement, with the originating 1920 papers cited bibliographically and the
  Bazett attribution caveat (Cobos Gil 2008; Taran & Szilagyi 1947) in the docstring. Van de Water may be
  implemented as `QTc = QT − 0.087 × (RR − 1000)` (RR in ms) citing the Van de Water 1989 abstract, flagged
  as derived in anaesthetised dogs.
- Report exactly what S7B Q&A 3.5 requires: absolute means, mean percent change from baseline, CIs and
  p-values vs baseline and vs vehicle; PK summary (Cmax, AUC, Tmax, parent + metabolites) with
  concentration–time plots; **individual animal data**.
- Assay sensitivity (minimum detectable difference, positive-control response, historical variance
  consistency) is a first-class output. **No ms threshold may be hard-coded** — the 5 ms / 10 ms / 3× numbers
  in Q&A 3.4 are explicitly hypothetical and must be entered per study with their justification.
- Exposure: require at least one PK sample on the PD day (Q&A 3.2); flag when exposures do not cover the
  anticipated high clinical exposure (Q&A 3.2, S7B Q&A 1.1).
- The QT liability call stays a Study Director determination (per `docs/14` row A10).
