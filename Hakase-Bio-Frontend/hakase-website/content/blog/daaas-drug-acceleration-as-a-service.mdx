Seat-based pricing made sense when software was a productivity tool. It makes
less sense when "the software" is doing a chunk of the scientific work.

Drug-Acceleration-as-a-Service (DAaaS) is our way of pricing Hakase the way
teams actually consume it: per deliverable, per stage, per trial.

## What that means in practice

- A discovery team pays for **a falsifier-backed lead package**, not a year of
  platform access.
- A clinical team pays for **a protocol intelligence report and a Monte Carlo
  enrollment plan**, not 40 named users.
- A commercial team pays for **a launch-readiness scorecard per indication**, not
  a CRM extension.

Each deliverable carries its evidence pack and confirmation slots. Each one is
tenant-isolated by default.

## Why this aligns incentives

When the engine is paid for outcomes, the team building the engine has to care
about outcomes. That's not a slogan — it's an architectural commitment that
shows up in how we gate predictions, how we collect confirmations, and how
quickly priors update across the foundation models.
