The default mode of clinical AI is "score and serve" — a model ranks options
and the team picks one. The model is rarely told whether the chosen option
actually worked.

Hakase Clinical inverts that. Every protocol intelligence output ships with:

- **A predicted outcome distribution** (e.g. P10 / P50 / P90 enrollment timelines).
- **A confirmation slot** — the real outcome that will eventually land.
- **A falsifier** — the condition under which the prediction is wrong.

When the real readouts arrive, they slot in automatically. The model improves
where it should and admits where it shouldn't.

## What changes for sponsors

- Site selection stops being a static ranking and becomes a confidence-tracked
  recommendation.
- Safety signal detection (PRR / ROR, Evans 2001 WHO) runs continuously against
  20M+ FAERS reports, with confirmation pairs.
- GO / NO-GO recommendations carry their own evidence ladder — auditable for
  regulators and defensible for boards.

## The economic argument

Falsifier-first AI is more expensive to build and cheaper to operate. It costs
more because every prediction needs a confirmation contract. It costs less
because drift is caught early and the team stops paying for false confidence.
