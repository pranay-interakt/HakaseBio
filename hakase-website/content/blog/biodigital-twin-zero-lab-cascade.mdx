A BioDigital Twin is not one model. It is a *cascade* — and the trick is keeping
that cascade falsifiable end-to-end.

Hakase Bio organizes the cascade into three layers:

- **L1 — Cellular AI Sim.** Mechanistic plus ML cell-state simulation across
  perturbations. Priors come from single-cell foundation models, scVI-integrated
  references, and curated pathway maps.
- **L2 — Structural × Tissue Gate.** Proteina/Boltz structures only enter the
  decision if the tissue-expression context supports them. We refuse to ship a
  structural prediction without its tissue gate.
- **L3 — Organism PBPK.** Whole-body PK/PD with IVIVE translation, so the
  first-in-human dose has an explicit translation argument behind it.

The cascade only earns its keep when each layer's prediction is paired with a
falsifier — a wet-lab assay, an animal readout, or a clinical observation that
would invalidate it. That pairing is enforced by the gate service, not by
goodwill.

## Why "predict-then-confirm" matters

We've watched too many platforms ship beautiful predictions with no slot for
the wet-lab result to land. The result is a model that quietly drifts from
reality. The predict-then-confirm contract makes that drift visible, ranked,
and addressable.

## What this looks like in practice

For a typical discovery program, the BioDigital Twin shrinks the screening
funnel from thousands of hypotheses to a few high-conviction leads — each one
carrying its falsifier. Confirmation data flows back as ground truth, not
training data, and the cascade tightens accordingly.
