# Chapter 1 — Purpose & Scope

## 1.1 Purpose

The Report & Results Specification defines the canonical scientific output presented to users following completion of a HakaseAI analysis.

It establishes:

- the structure of computational reports;
- the scientific variables presented;
- visualization requirements;
- evidence presentation standards;
- confidence communication;
- uncertainty reporting;
- traceability requirements;
- downloadable assets.

This document serves as the authoritative contract between the Decision & Validation Framework and the Presentation Layer.

---

## 1.2 Scope

This specification governs every report generated by HakaseAI regardless of therapeutic modality.

It applies to:

- Peptide Discovery
- Therapeutic Peptides
- Antimicrobial Peptides
- Cell-Penetrating Peptides
- Future antibody workflows
- Future RNA workflows
- Future protein therapeutics

---

## 1.3 Design Principles

Every report SHALL be:

- scientifically transparent;
- fully traceable;
- evidence-driven;
- reproducible;
- technology-independent;
- understandable without hiding uncertainty.

Reports SHALL summarize scientific evidence.

Reports SHALL NOT generate new scientific predictions.

# Chapter 22 — Reference Ranges & Interpretive Thresholds

## 22.1 Purpose and Governance

This chapter defines the literature-derived reference ranges, interpretive thresholds, and source citations used to help a reader interpret each displayed variable.

These ranges are interpretive aids for the Presentation Layer only. They are **not** the GO / Conditional GO / NO-GO gating logic — that logic is owned exclusively by the Decision & Reasoning Framework Specification (Policy Objects, Policy Resolution). Where a value below duplicates a Decision Engine threshold, the Decision & Reasoning Framework Specification is the authoritative source and this chapter SHALL be kept consistent with it.

Three categories of range appear below, and the report SHALL label which category applies to a given displayed value:

- **Hard cutoff** — a fixed threshold defined in the originating peer-reviewed method (e.g. instability index, pLDDT, TM-score, NetMHCpan %Rank). Reproducible and tool-independent.
- **Typical/comparative range** — a range observed across characterized peptides of a given class (e.g. AMP net charge, Boman index). Descriptive, not diagnostic; excursions outside the range are not necessarily disqualifying.
- **Model-specific operating point** — a default classification threshold set by a specific tool's training/validation procedure (e.g. ToxinPred2, HemoPI-style hemolysis classifiers). Changes with model version and SHALL be versioned per Chapter 15 (Computational Provenance).

Where no defensible universal range exists (e.g. molecular weight, manufacturability score), the report SHALL state this explicitly rather than imply a cutoff that doesn't exist — consistent with the Chapter 1 principle of not hiding uncertainty.

## 22.2 Physicochemical Reference Ranges

|Parameter|Reference Range / Threshold|Category|Interpretation|Source|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|Molecular Weight|No universal cutoff; peptide therapeutics commonly span ~500–5,000 Da|Typical range|Larger peptides generally show lower oral bioavailability and higher synthesis complexity; route-dependent|General peptide-therapeutics literature; no single canonical source|
|Isoelectric Point (pI)|No pass/fail threshold|Typical range|Solubility risk rises when pI is close to the formulation/physiological pH (~7.4), since net charge — and thus solubility — is minimal at pI|General protein-solubility theory|
|Net Charge|AMPs typically +2 to +9|Typical range|Higher cationic charge correlates with electrostatic attraction to anionic microbial membranes; not a pass/fail gate|Representative characterized AMPs, e.g. net charge +5 (Wijesekara et al. 2020, Octominin) and +8 (Zhu et al. 2020, SM-985)|
|GRAVY Score|Negative = net hydrophilic; positive = net hydrophobic; no fixed cutoff|Typical range|Sign indicates dominant character; magnitude is context-dependent on modality (e.g. CPPs and membrane-disrupting AMPs tolerate less negative values)|Kyte & Doolittle, 1982, _J. Mol. Biol._ 157:105–132|
|Hydrophobic Moment (µH)|~0.3–0.6 typical for amphipathic α-helical AMPs|Typical range|Higher µH indicates stronger amphipathicity, associated with membrane-active peptides|Eisenberg et al., 1984, _PNAS_ 81:140–144|
|Instability Index|< 40 = predicted stable; > 40 = predicted unstable|Hard cutoff|Dipeptide-composition-based stability estimate in a test tube (not in vivo half-life)|Guruprasad, Reddy & Pandit, 1990, _Protein Eng._ 4(2):155–161|
|Aliphatic Index|No fixed cutoff; higher value = greater relative thermostability (comparative)|Typical range|Relative volume occupied by aliphatic side chains (Ala, Val, Ile, Leu)|Ikai, 1980, _J. Biochem._ 88(6):1895–1898|
|Boman Index|< 2.48 kcal/mol → membrane-active/AMP-like; ~2.53–3.04 kcal/mol → multifunctional, high protein-binding potential; 0–3 kcal/mol is the typical range reported across characterized AMPs|Typical range|Estimates a peptide's propensity to bind other proteins vs. partition into membranes; derived from Radzicka & Wolfenden (1988) solubility data|Boman, 2003, _J. Intern. Med._ 254(3):197–215; range corroborated in silico across AMP libraries (e.g. anti-schistosomal AMP screening, ScienceDirect 2021)|
|Aromaticity|No fixed cutoff|Typical range|Higher aromatic residue content raises UV-quantifiability but can also raise aggregation/oxidation risk|General peptide chemistry; no single canonical source|

## 22.3 Structural Biology Reference Ranges

|Parameter|Reference Range / Threshold|Category|Interpretation|Source|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|pLDDT|≥ 90 very high confidence; 70–90 confident; 50–70 low confidence; < 50 very low confidence (often disordered)|Hard cutoff|Per-residue backbone/side-chain accuracy tiers|Jumper et al., 2021, _Nature_ 596:583–589; EBI AlphaFold training materials|
|Predicted TM-score|< 0.17 statistically indistinguishable from random; 0.17–0.5 ambiguous; > 0.5 same fold; > 0.7 high-confidence same SCOP/CATH family|Hard cutoff|Length-independent measure of global fold similarity|Zhang & Skolnick, 2004, _Proteins_ 57:702–710; Xu & Zhang, 2010, _Bioinformatics_ 26:889–895|
|Docking Score / Binding Energy Estimate|No universal cutoff; more negative (kcal/mol) indicates stronger predicted affinity; drug-like complexes commonly fall around −6 to −12 kcal/mol on the AutoDock Vina scale|Model-specific operating point|Comparative ranking metric only — not a measured thermodynamic binding constant, and not comparable across different docking engines|AutoDock Vina scoring convention (Trott & Olson, 2010, _J. Comput. Chem._ 31:455–461); treat as a heuristic, not a hard gate|
|Disorder Probability|No fixed cutoff|Typical range|Correlates inversely with regions of pLDDT < 50|Tunyasuvunakool et al., 2021, _Nature_ 596:590–596|

## 22.4 Functional Analysis Reference Ranges

|Parameter|Reference Range / Threshold|Category|Interpretation|Source|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|Functional Probability / Target Interaction Score|No universal cutoff; threshold is set at the underlying classifier's own validated operating point|Model-specific operating point|Reported alongside the model version that defines it, per Chapter 15|Model-specific — see Functional Analysis Scientific Service Specification|
|Similarity Scores|No fixed cutoff|Typical range|Higher sequence/embedding similarity to validated actives raises confidence but is corroborating evidence, not causal proof of activity|FAISS / MMseqs2 embedding-search context|

## 22.5 Developability Reference Ranges

|Parameter|Reference Range / Threshold|Category|Interpretation|Source|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|Aggregation Propensity (A3D score)|Positive = aggregation-prone; negative = solubility-promoting; more negative = more soluble|Hard cutoff (sign convention)|Structure-based, per-residue and whole-chain aggregation propensity; magnitude is comparative, not absolute|Zambrano et al., 2015, _NAR_ 43:W306–W313; Kuriata et al., 2019, _NAR_ 47:W300–W307 (Aggrescan3D 2.0)|
|Protease Stability|No universal cutoff|Typical range|Comparative half-life estimate; flagged primarily by presence of known protease-susceptible motifs|Tool/model-specific|
|Manufacturability / Synthesis Complexity|No fixed cutoff|Platform-internal heuristic|Scored from residue count, cyclization, and non-canonical residue content|Platform-internal scoring convention — not derived from a single external source|

## 22.6 Safety Reference Ranges

|Parameter|Reference Range / Threshold|Category|Interpretation|Source|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|Toxicity Score|ToxinPred2 default classification threshold: 0.6 (AAC/RF model); score above threshold → predicted toxic|Model-specific operating point|Threshold is tunable per deployment; report SHALL display the threshold actually used, not assume the default|Sharma et al., 2022, _Brief. Bioinform._ 23(5):bbac174; raghavagps/toxinpred2|
|Immunogenicity Risk (NetMHCpan %Rank)|MHC-I default: Strong Binder %Rank < 0.5; Weak Binder %Rank < 2|Hard cutoff (tool default, adjustable)|Lower %Rank = higher predicted probability of MHC presentation, i.e. higher immunogenicity risk|Reynisson et al., 2020, _Nucleic Acids Res._ 48:W449–W454 (NetMHCpan-4.1)|
|Hemolysis Probability|No single universal cutoff across tools; classifier probability with a typical default operating threshold of ~0.5|Model-specific operating point|Threshold is training-set and tool specific; report SHALL cite the exact tool and threshold used|Tool-specific (e.g. HemoPI-class classifiers)|
|Allergenicity|No fixed numeric cutoff|Model-specific|Typically alignment/motif-based match against curated allergen databases rather than a continuous score|Tool-specific (e.g. AllergenOnline-style database matching)|

## 22.7 Reporting Requirement

For every parameter listed above, the report SHALL display, alongside the value:

- the reference range or threshold applied (if any);
- the category (Hard cutoff / Typical range / Model-specific operating point);
- the source citation, or an explicit statement that no universal reference range exists.

This SHALL be traceable per Chapter 16 (Evidence Traceability) back to the Evidence Object and model version that produced the value.

## Final Architectural Principle

The Report & Results Specification is the **sole interface between HakaseAI's scientific computation and the end user**. It does not perform prediction, inference, or decision-making. Instead, it faithfully communicates validated computational evidence, preserves uncertainty and provenance, and ensures that every displayed conclusion is traceable to its originating Evidence Objects. The report is therefore a transparent scientific communication artifact, not an independent analytical component.
